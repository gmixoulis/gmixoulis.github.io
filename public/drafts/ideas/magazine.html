George Michoulis

George Michoulis is a blockchain developer and researcher in Thessaloniki, Greece. He writes smart contracts, studies ledgers with graph-based machine learning, builds full-stack Web3 systems, and has taught networks and security at university level.

As told to this page, September 2026

On the cover: a crane in sumi ink, from the brush set used across this site.

A diploma you can check

Thessaloniki is home. I live here and work remotely, and most of my work is on blockchains: smart contracts, graph-based machine learning, and the full-stack Web3 systems that put both in front of people.

I came to blockchains through a practical problem: how do you check that a diploma is real? I studied Applied Informatics at the University of Macedonia from 2015 to 2020, and my thesis looked at verifying academic credentials on Ethereum. I kept at the question after I graduated, and in 2020 and 2021 I also held a Basic Research grant from the university.

From that work came VerDe, a system for verifying academic qualifications on Ethereum. I introduced it at the XIV Balkan Conference on Operational Research in 2020. In 2022 I followed it with a process-aware approach to the same problem, published in Simulation Modelling Practice and Theory. That paper has been cited 32 times, more than anything else I have written.

For my master’s I moved across town to the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki and its Data and Web Science programme, from 2020 to 2022, on a DeepMind scholarship. There I worked on graph-embedding methods for detecting fraud on blockchains. A ledger is a graph of accounts and transactions, and embeddings turn that graph into something a model can learn from.

Three sides of the work

I think of my work as having three sides: research, building and teaching. Research means publications, and startup work, on how effective ledgers are in practice and on what actually changes when a blockchain system is put to use.

Building means Web3, from NFTs to DeFi; data science with graph embeddings; and shipping across Solidity, TypeScript and the cloud. Since May 2026 I have been a Web3 full-stack developer at Cyberscope by TAC, where I make Web3 products end to end: the contracts, the on-chain integrations, React and Next frontends, and security tooling.

Before that, from 2024 to 2026, I was a blockchain developer and researcher at Sidroco Holdings. I worked on an NFT marketplace for 5G, prototypes on Hyperledger Fabric, an Erasmus+ learning management system and multinational EU projects. From 2022 to 2024 I was at the University of Nicosia and IFF, building large Next.js, NestJS and Docker applications and a Web3 NFT marketplace.

Teaching is the third side. From 2023 to 2025 I was a lecturer and academic partner for the University of Derby at Mediterranean College, teaching Networks and Security, Web Scripting and Application Development, and I designed the assessment to be inclusive first. I also speak in public about Web3 and edge technology.

On the record

Much of what I have published is about three things on blockchains: verification, governance and markets. After the credential papers came a framework for decentralised governance, applied to Compound Finance, at the Mathematical Research for Blockchain Economy conference in 2023. FraMark, a blockchain marketplace for 5G network management built on fractional NFTs, appeared at the World Symposium on Communication Engineering in 2024, and a survey of blockchain marketplaces for 5G network slicing followed at IEEE ICCE in 2025.

Not everything fits that pattern. My MSc work includes a paper on graph embeddings and node features for predicting drug–target interactions, and I have written about blockchain in higher education for the Summit on Gender Equality in Computing, and about data security for smart cities.

There were awards along the way: the DeepMind scholarship that funded my master’s, third place at the Infinitech Hackathon in June 2022, and in 2025 an award at the first Move and Sui bootcamp in Thessaloniki. I keep the certificates, too. There are thirty-four scans in the archive, and six of them are reproduced here.

Some of what I have built is easier to show than to describe: sites for university groups and projects, a few of which appear below.
